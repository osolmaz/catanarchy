# Terra seed 47 run report at thinking max

This report covers two games on board seed 47 with `openai/gpt-5.6-terra` at thinking level `max` against `huggingface/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash:novita` at thinking level `high`, in swapped seats. It is the third pair in the seed 47 series, after Luna at `high` and Luna at `xhigh`.

DeepSeek won both games. DeepSeek has now won all six seed 47 games.

An earlier terra pair ran at thinking `high` for 18 minutes and was stopped, because the deepest level was requested. Its measurements carry the level comparison below, and its packages are published as partial runs.

The terra seats outran the time available in a turn. That is the main new finding in this report. In game B it cost terra 23 board decisions.

A correction on 2026-09-13 shows where the time went. A turn key holds two pools: the exploration pool, which is the turn window, and the finalization pool, which is the grace period. In game B the empty finalization pool caused 21 of the 34 empty attempts and the exhausted exploration window caused 13. The correction section below lists the follow-up games that establish this.

Two games on one seed are a behavioural case study. They are not a strength verdict. About 85 games are needed to detect a win-rate shift from 50% to 65%.

## The time pools limited the terra seats

Game B recorded 48 failed decisions against 3 in game A. Every failure is category `agent-error`.

| Measure                            | Game A | Game B |
| ---------------------------------- | -----: | -----: |
| Failed decisions                   |      3 |     48 |
| Failed game decisions              |      0 |     23 |
| Failed negotiation decisions       |      3 |     25 |
| Failed decisions on terra seats    |      1 |     40 |
| Failed decisions on DeepSeek seats |      2 |      8 |

The failures split into three groups.

**34 attempts never reached the model.** They recorded zero tokens for input and output, and every one is a terra seat. 31 of them returned in 0 to 1 ms. The decision path returns an empty response without a request when a time pool is empty, so the attempt fails at once and the harness plays the fallback action, which is the first legal action.

A turn key holds two time pools. The exploration pool is the turn window. The finalization pool is the grace period. Both start again when the key changes, and the game and negotiation decisions of one turn share the key. The recorded time before each empty attempt separates the two causes. In game B, 13 of the 34 attempts happened after the seat had spent the whole 600 s window inside that key, so the exploration pool was empty. The other 21 happened with 0 to 574 s spent in the key, which leaves time in the window, so the finalization pool was empty. The trace did not record the error text at the time, so this split comes from the recorded clock. `docs/AGENT_HARNESS.md` now covers both pools, and the harness records the error text in `failureMessage` on the failed attempt and on the fallback that follows it.

**14 attempts received a response but produced no legal action.** Terra failed 6 of them and DeepSeek failed 8. These are ordinary selection failures, and they appear in the earlier pairs at a similar rate.

Terra lost 23 board decisions in game B to a fallback action, and none in game A. DeepSeek lost no board decision in either game. Terra's reasoning filled the time pools of game B, so its later decisions in those turns were replaced by the first legal action.

This is a harness setting, not a model error. The 300 s grace period in the later games removes it. A longer turn window does not, because the finalization pool is separate and small.

### Correction: the finalization grace, not the turn window

This report first named the turn window as the cause and said that a longer window removes it. The follow-up games below show that this is wrong.

| Game                        | Turn window | Grace | Empty exploration | Empty finalization | No legal action |
| --------------------------- | ----------: | ----: | ----------------: | -----------------: | --------------: |
| `terra-max-seed47-a`        |       600 s |  60 s |                 0 |                  0 |               3 |
| `terra-max-seed47-b`        |       600 s |  60 s |                13 |                 21 |              14 |
| `terra-max30-seed47-a`      |      1800 s |  60 s |                 0 |                 18 |              21 |
| `terra-max30-seed47-b`      |      1800 s |  60 s |                 0 |                 23 |              22 |
| `terra-max30-g300-seed47-a` |      1800 s | 300 s |                 0 |                  0 |              13 |
| `terra-max30-g300-seed47-b` |      1800 s | 300 s |                 0 |                  0 |               5 |

A six-times longer turn window left the empty attempts in place and moved every one of them to the finalization pool. Raising the grace to 300 s removed all of them. Every other game in the later set, including the four sol games and the terra `high` pair, reached zero on both pools at a 300 s grace. One single empty finalization attempt remains in the terra `high` pair at a 60 s grace, in game B.

The cause is simple. A model that writes prose instead of calling the selection tool is re-prompted inside the finalization pool, and that pool holds only the grace period. When it empties, every later decision of that seat in the same turn key gets no model call at all. A larger turn window does not refill it.

## Was the setup fair to terra

Every rule was the same in both games: the turn window, the context window, the planning steps, the negotiation rounds, the decision bound, and the board. Each model holds each of the four seats once across the pair. Terra held 39 and 40 opening pips, and DeepSeek held 40 and 39. Both models compacted their sessions, and DeepSeek compacted more often in game B, 6 and 5 times against 3 and 3. The sessions contain no provider error.

Terra was not behind when the failures began. In game B it led 11 to 9 at turn 40, and the heavy failures started at turn 56. In game A terra never led, and it lost only three decisions. So the time limits did not decide either game.

One feature is not neutral, and it is the turn time budget. Terra needs much longer in the tail of its decisions, so the same time buys it fewer decisions.

| Measure, game B                       |     Terra | DeepSeek |
| ------------------------------------- | --------: | -------: |
| Decisions                             |       300 |      267 |
| Median decision time                  |   3845 ms |  4413 ms |
| 90th percentile                       |  48073 ms | 28465 ms |
| Time in decisions                     | 122.3 min | 48.4 min |
| Share of the wall clock               |     71.6% |    28.4% |
| Requests that never reached the model |        20 |        0 |

The sessions show the missing requests. Terra's two seats made 260 requests, and the session files hold 240 decision prompts. All 20 missing prompts are on terra seats, 17 on orange and 3 on blue. The DeepSeek seats have no missing prompt. The turn clock steering message, `[Turn clock] n seconds remain. Select soon.`, also reached only the terra seats: 4 times on orange and 2 times on blue.

Turn 56 of game B shows one whole turn. Orange, a terra seat, spent 290990 ms and 283416 ms on two decisions. Its turn clock then stood at 574407 ms of the 600000 ms window. The next request timed out at 25596 ms with no tokens. Every request after it returned in 0 ms without reaching the model, while its clock ran from 600003 ms to 600007 ms. The harness played the fallback action nine times in that turn, including three development-card choices, a robber move, a maritime trade, a road, and the end of the turn. In the same negotiation window the other three seats decided normally, at 3039 ms, 4647 ms, and 3901 ms.

The measured terra result is a lower bound, not a fair measure of terra at this level. A comparison that wants to separate the models must give both models the same number of decisions, either by a larger finalization grace or by a decision count instead of a wall clock.

## Play observations

These are observations from two games. They are not claims about model strength.

- Terra and DeepSeek each proposed about half the offers in game A, 21 and 21. In game B DeepSeek proposed 26 and terra 19. The market accepted 9 of 42 offers in game A and 5 of 45 in game B.
- Game A offered 7 counteroffers. Game B offered none. The two earlier mixed games offered 15 and 6.
- Free-form messages were common: 24 in game A and 27 in game B, against 5 in the original seed 47 game.
- Both games built more than the earlier mixed games. Game A built 4 cities and 7 settlements. Game B built 8 cities and 11 settlements, and the winner held 13 roads.
- Maritime trades were low in game A at 8 and high in game B at 20. Terra used the port more than Luna did in the earlier pairs.
- The robber took from the eventual winner four times in game B. In game A the most robbed seat was red, a terra seat, which finished on 4 points.
- Terra decided faster than DeepSeek in the game phase, at 3675 ms and 2560 ms against 4435 ms and 4956 ms. DeepSeek decided faster in negotiation in game B, at 4125 ms against 6322 ms.
- Both games ended without a revealed victory-point card, and neither game awarded Largest Army.

## Limits

- Two games on one seed cannot support a strength claim. Terra lost both games, and so did Luna at both levels. The sample is too small to separate the models.
- The time-pool finding rests on game B. Game A shows almost none of it, and both games ended within 65 turns. A later correction moved the larger share from the exploration pool to the finalization pool, and the 300 s grace removed both.
- The failure traces do not record the error text or the budget key, so the cause of the 31 instant failures is inferred from the decision path, the zero token counts, and the time already spent in the turn.
- The cost ceiling for a game is $250, and the harness stops a game at $37.84 of real spend, because it reserves its own worst-case estimate for the next request. Neither game came near that stop.
- The stopped `high` pair is partial. It is published as a partial run, and its figures describe 18 minutes of play.
- These games ran one configuration on one machine with Pi 0.85.1.
